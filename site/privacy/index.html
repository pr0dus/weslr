Short Summary

WESLR is a governed, continuity-first intelligence system under development. This Privacy Policy explains how personal data may be processed when people visit the WESLR website, contact WESLR, apply for pilot access, use invited tester accounts, use future workspace/chat functions, upload files, create artifacts, use future payment/subscription functions, or participate in a future marketplace.

As of this revision, weslr.de is publicly reachable as a static WESLR website. staging.weslr.de and chat.weslr.de are password protected. Public self-registration is not open, the public pilot is not live, no public paid product is live, file uploads are not enabled for public users, and no marketplace is live. Some sections of this Policy describe expected future functionality and apply only when those functions are enabled.

4. Website Delivery, Hosting, Security, and Logs

When a person visits WESLR websites, technical data may be processed to deliver the website, maintain security, prevent abuse, and troubleshoot errors. This may include:

  • IP address
  • Date and time of access
  • Requested URL/path
  • Referrer URL where available
  • Browser type and version
  • Operating system/device type
  • Language settings
  • Request headers and technical metadata
  • Security events and access logs
  • Error logs and performance metadata

Hosting/server provider: Owner-operated server infrastructure currently used for the WESLR website and protected routes.

Purpose: Website delivery, secure operation, fraud/abuse prevention, debugging, staging protection, and infrastructure reliability.

Legal basis: Article 6(1)(f) GDPR — legitimate interests in providing a secure, functional website and protecting WESLR systems. Where processing is necessary to perform a contract or take steps before entering a contract, Article 6(1)(b) GDPR may apply. Where legal obligations apply, Article 6(1)(c) GDPR may apply.

Retention: Default operational retention target is up to 6 months unless shorter deletion is requested/required or longer retention is required for legal obligations, security, abuse prevention, accounting, disputes, backups, or legal claims.

WESLR currently uses Cloudflare for DNS, domain routing, Cloudflare Tunnel / reverse-proxy routing, HTTPS/TLS-related delivery support, security/abuse protection where enabled, and Cloudflare Email Routing. Cloudflare Analytics is expected to be added for website traffic observation.

Cloudflare processing may include:

  • DNS and routing metadata
  • Visitor IP addresses and request metadata
  • Security logs and abuse-prevention signals
  • Caching metadata if caching is enabled
  • Cloudflare Tunnel or access-control metadata if used
  • Email Routing metadata for emails sent to WESLR addresses
  • Technical logs needed to operate Cloudflare services
  • Cloudflare Analytics or traffic measurement data if enabled

Cloudflare role: Processor/controller role must be confirmed from the active Cloudflare service configuration and Cloudflare terms; for this draft, Cloudflare is treated as a provider involved in DNS, Tunnel/routing, security-related metadata, and Email Routing.

International transfers: Cloudflare may process metadata outside the EU/EEA depending on service configuration. WESLR must confirm applicable Cloudflare Data Processing Addendum, Standard Contractual Clauses, EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework status if relied upon, and any supplementary measures before public launch.

7. Pilot Interest and Invitation-Based Access

At the time of this draft, no public WESLR pilot is live unless explicitly launched later. Pilot access is expected to be invitation-based. Public self-registration will be blocked initially. The first 50 tester accounts may be created manually by WESLR/operator/admin.

There is no automatic right to access WESLR. Submitting a request or showing interest does not guarantee invitation, account creation, pilot participation, commercial access, or continued access.

WESLR may process pilot-interest data such as: name, email address, role or company, intended use case, region/country, technical context, requested access type, notes and correspondence, invitation status and decision metadata.

Legal bases: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (pre-contractual steps), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interests in selecting suitable testers and managing pilot capacity).

Retention: Pilot inquiries are generally retained for up to 6 months after the last interaction or pilot decision, unless the person becomes a tester/user, asks to remain on a waiting list, or legal/security/business reasons require longer retention.

8. User Accounts

When user accounts are enabled, WESLR may process account data such as: name and/or display name, email address, password hash or authentication credentials, account status, role and permissions, organization or workspace affiliation, invitation status, login metadata, security logs, preferences and settings, billing status if payment features are enabled, account lifecycle events.

Initially, public self-registration should be blocked. The first tester accounts may be created manually by WESLR/operator/admin, for example for the first 50 invited testers.

Inactive accounts: Intended to be scheduled for deletion after 6 months of inactivity. WESLR should send an automatic email warning before inactivity deletion where reasonably possible, with the exact warning period to be implemented before account launch. Deletion remains subject to exceptions for legal obligations, tax/accounting records, marketplace transaction records, security logs, abuse prevention, disputes, backups, and legal claims.

Legal bases: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (account creation, access management, service delivery), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (security, fraud prevention, abuse prevention), Article 6(1)(c) GDPR (legal obligations where applicable).

9. Chat and Workspace Functionality

When chat and workspace functionality is enabled, WESLR may process data needed to provide WESLR functionality, including: user prompts/messages, conversation history, workspace/project names, user instructions/preferences, selected context, system outputs, generated drafts, operator decisions or approvals, timestamps, audit logs, feedback, moderation/safety/security flags, technical metadata.

WESLR positioning: WESLR is not an LLM and not a chatbot wrapper. WESLR is positioned as a governed, continuity-first intelligence system. Models or LLMs may be optional capability providers, not WESLR's identity or authority.

AI/model providers: WESLR is intended to provide configuration possibilities for user-configurable or workspace-configurable AI/model providers through help/wiki or similar documentation. WESLR does not provide one fixed default AI/model provider by default and does not rely on one model provider as its identity. If WESLR later offers any default provider, that provider must be listed and legally reviewed before activation.

Where a user or workspace administrator selects or configures an AI/model provider, data sent to that provider is processed under that provider's terms, privacy policy, data-processing settings, and retention/training configuration. WESLR provides configuration possibilities and governance controls, but users/workspace administrators remain responsible for selecting providers suitable for their data, use case, and legal obligations.

Legal bases: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the requested WESLR service), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (security, quality assurance, debugging, abuse prevention), Article 6(1)(a) GDPR (consent where optional features require consent).

Retention: Chat/workspace data is generally retained while the account/workspace is active and is intended to be deleted after account deletion or 6 months of inactivity, subject to user controls, backups, legal obligations, security, marketplace/payment records, disputes, or legal claims.

10. Memory, Continuity, Context, Evidence, and Provenance

WESLR may include memory, continuity, context, evidence, provenance, and approval-record features. These are expected to support continuity over time, maintain context where appropriate, preserve source/provenance relationships, and record operator or user decisions.

Expected data may include: bounded user memory/context records, workspace continuity metadata, user-approved contextual memory, project-specific memory, evidence and provenance references, source references and citation metadata, operator approval records, decision metadata, change history and audit metadata, deletion/export request records.

WESLR should not remember everything by default. Memory/context retention should be bounded, purposeful, transparent, and controllable. Before launch, WESLR must define:

  • What data can become memory/context
  • Whether memory is per-user, per-workspace, per-organization, or global
  • How users can view, edit, export, or delete memory/context
  • How evidence/provenance records are retained
  • How operator approval records are retained
  • What happens after account deletion
  • Backup deletion delay
  • Whether certain legal/security records must remain

Legal bases: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing continuity features), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (maintaining secure, reliable operation), Article 6(1)(a) GDPR (consent where optional memory features require consent).

File upload functionality is planned/future unless explicitly enabled. Files should not be uploaded unless the feature is active and users have accepted applicable Terms of Use and this Privacy Policy.

When file uploads are enabled, WESLR may process: uploaded file content, file names, file types, file sizes, file metadata, extracted text, thumbnails or previews, derived summaries, analysis results, version history, security scan results, access permissions and sharing settings, storage and backup metadata, deletion/export metadata.

WESLR may use malware scanning, abuse detection, content validation, and access controls to protect users and systems.

Storage provider: Owner-operated server infrastructure unless a future storage provider is added after review.

Users must not upload personal data, confidential data, third-party data, special-category data, trade secrets, regulated data, or unlawful content unless they have the legal right and lawful basis to do so and the relevant WESLR feature and terms permit it.

Legal bases: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing upload, analysis, storage, export features), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (security scanning, abuse prevention), Article 6(1)(c) GDPR (legal obligations where applicable).

If payment functions are enabled, WESLR may process: subscription/payment status, plan, product, or marketplace purchase details, invoice data, billing address, tax/VAT data, payment method token or last four digits if provided by the payment processor, transaction IDs, refunds, chargebacks, failed payment records, fraud prevention and risk signals, accounting/legal retention records.

Payment provider: Subject to change / to be confirmed before payment activation.

Legal bases: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (processing payments, subscriptions, invoices, refunds, and contracts), Article 6(1)(c) GDPR (tax, accounting, commercial, and legal retention obligations), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (fraud prevention, payment security, dispute handling).

15. AI/Model Provider Processing

WESLR is not an LLM and not a chatbot wrapper. WESLR is a governed, continuity-first intelligence system. Models/LLMs may be optional capability providers, not WESLR's identity or authority.

When AI-enabled features are enabled, WESLR may use AI/model providers to process: prompts/messages, uploaded file content or extracted text, artifacts, workspace/project context, memory/context selected for the specific task, evidence/provenance references, user instructions/preferences, system outputs and feedback, technical metadata required for operation, debugging, safety, or security.

AI/model providers: User-configurable or workspace-configurable AI/model providers where supported. WESLR does not provide fixed default providers by default. Any future WESLR-provided defaults must be listed before activation.

Draft default commitments before final legal/product confirmation:

  • WESLR does not use public website visitor data to train models.
  • For account/workspace data, training or product-improvement use requires separate documentation, controls, provider review, and legal review before launch.
  • WESLR should not use account/workspace data to train WESLR or third-party models by default unless the user has been clearly informed and an appropriate legal basis and controls exist.
  • If anonymized or aggregated telemetry is used for service improvement, WESLR must define what data is collected, whether it can reasonably identify users, how it is aggregated/anonymized, retention, opt-out if applicable, and provider access.

Legal bases: Article 6(1)(b) GDPR (where AI/model processing is necessary to provide requested functionality), Article 6(1)(f) GDPR (security, debugging, abuse prevention, and quality improvement within strict limits), Article 6(1)(a) GDPR (optional training, optional model-improvement, optional personalization, optional analytics, or optional cross-workspace features if consent is required/used).
