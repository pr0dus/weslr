# CircleForge - Philosophical References

## Major Philosophical Traditions

### Western Philosophy

**Ancient Greek:**
- **Presocratic Philosophy** - Rational explanations of cosmos (Thales, Anaximander, Heraclitus, Parmenides, Democritus)
- **Socratic Method** - Critical inquiry through dialogue, moral philosophy, recognizing ignorance
- **Platonism** - Eternal Forms/Ideas as ultimate reality
- **Aristotelianism** - Empirical observation, substance, causality, virtue ethics
- **Stoicism** - Virtue = living in accordance with nature/reason
- **Epicureanism** - Pleasure (ataraxia) as highest good
- **Skepticism** - Suspension of judgment (epoché)

**Medieval:**
- **Scholasticism** - Medieval Christian philosophy (Aquinas, Anselm)
- **Islamic Golden Age Philosophy** - Avicenna, Averroes, synthesis of Greek/Islamic thought

**Modern:**
- **Rationalism** (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz) - Reason as primary knowledge source
- **Empiricism** (Locke, Berkeley, Hume) - Experience as primary knowledge source
- **Kantianism** - Synthesis of rationalism/empiricism, categorical imperative
- **Utilitarianism** (Bentham, Mill) - Greatest happiness principle
- **Pragmatism** (Peirce, James, Dewey) - Truth as what works in practice
- **German Idealism** (Hegel, Fichte, Schelling) - Reality as mental construction

**20th Century:**
- **Phenomenology** (Husserl) - Study of conscious experience
- **Existentialism** (Sartre, Camus, Heidegger) - Individual existence, freedom, meaning
- **Analytic Philosophy** - Logic, language, science
- **Continental Philosophy** - History, culture, critique
- **Structuralism/Post-structuralism** (Saussure, Foucault, Derrida) - Structures of meaning
- **Postmodernism** - Skepticism of grand narratives

### Eastern Philosophy

**Indian:**
- **Hindu Schools** (Vedanta, Nyaya, Yoga) - Brahman, Atman, karma, dharma
- **Buddhism** - Four Noble Truths, emptiness, mindfulness, Madhyamaka, Yogācāra
- **Jainism** - Ahimsa, Anekantavada

**Chinese:**
- **Confucianism** - Ethics, social harmony, filial piety
- **Daoism (Taoism)** - Wu-wei (non-action), simplicity, balance
- **Legalism** - Strict laws, state control
- **Chan/Zen Buddhism** - Meditation, direct insight, koans

**Japanese:**
- **Zen Buddhism** - Zazen meditation, sudden enlightenment
- **Bushido** - Warrior code, honor, loyalty

### Key Concepts

**Metaphysics:**
- Substance, causality, identity, change, time, space

**Epistemology:**
- Knowledge vs. belief, justification, skepticism, truth

**Ethics:**
- Normative ethics, metaethics, applied ethics
- Virtue ethics, deontology, utilitarianism

**Logic:**
- Deduction, induction, abductive reasoning
- Formal systems, paradoxes

**Political Philosophy:**
- Justice, rights, governance, liberty, equality

## CircleForge Relevance

The CircleForge system draws on multiple philosophical traditions:

1. **Aristotelian Virtue Ethics** - Traits like empathy, trust, integrity
2. **Stoic Resilience** - Emotional regulation, adaptability
3. **Buddhist Mindfulness** - Self-awareness, attention regulation
4. **Pragmatic Learning** - Learning from experience, adaptation
5. **Phenomenological Reflection** - Self-continuity, memory integration
6. **Existential Choice** - Initiative, authenticity, responsibility

The mathematical behavior system implements these philosophies as:
- Differential equations for trait dynamics
- Feedback loops for self-correction
- Saturation limits for bounded growth
- Context-sensitive adaptation
