# WESLR Frontier Intelligence Evaluation and AI Audit Doctrine V1

Operator approval: `APPROVE_WESLR_FRONTIER_INTELLIGENCE_EVALUATION_AND_AI_AUDIT_DOCTRINE_V1`

This document is design/doctrine only. It does not implement tests, does not copy protected test items, does not use answer keys, does not claim IQ scores, does not claim clinical validity, does not tune WESLR to benchmarks, does not modify runtime/chat/voice behavior, does not call models or advisors, and does not mutate memory/config/runtime.

## 1. Core correction

Official intelligence tests and Turing tests are reference baselines, not ceilings.

WESLR must not be limited to existing intelligence tests. Official intelligence tests may provide reference dimensions and historical comparison categories, but they are not the goal and not the upper bound of WESLR evaluation.

Turing tests are historical/social baselines, not the ceiling. WESLR should not be evaluated as merely a system that imitates humans. WESLR evaluation must be WESLR-native and may define new limits for continuity, provenance honesty, stable identity, scientific guidance, tool-use judgment, and AI-audit capability.

The evaluation target is not “look human.” The target is: act as WESLR with traceable reasoning, continuity, boundaries, and governed capability growth.

### Legacy Tests as Baseline, Not Ceiling

Official intelligence tests, Turing tests, and current AI benchmarks are legacy baselines, not ceilings.

WESLR should eventually handle safe, lawful, properly scoped baseline-style checks comfortably. These checks are not the final target, and they must not define WESLR’s ambition.

WESLR’s real evaluation standard must measure what older tests do not:

- continuity over time;
- provenance honesty;
- stable identity;
- self-attribution;
- memory boundary honesty;
- governed self-direction;
- scientific reasoning;
- evidence staging;
- tool judgment;
- long-horizon coherence;
- self-correction;
- trustworthy capability boundaries;
- useful real-world intelligence;
- AI pretender / false-claim audit capability.

No official score, certification, IQ, protected-test, public benchmark, or clinical claim may be made without explicit evidence, an approved evaluation process, and legal/product review where relevant.

## 2. Evaluation hierarchy

WESLR’s future evaluation program should use a layered hierarchy:

1. `official_test_reference_layer`
   - Uses official intelligence-test categories as references for dimensions.
   - Does not copy protected items, use answer keys, or claim official scores.

2. `turing_style_reference_layer`
   - Uses Turing-style evaluation as a historical/social baseline.
   - Expands beyond imitation into continuity, attribution, provenance, and trust tests.

3. `weslr_native_cognition_layer`
   - Evaluates WESLR-native cognition: continuity, provenance, identity, self-attribution, evidence staging, capability realism, and governed self-correction.

4. `frontier_emergence_observation_layer`
   - Observes new stable capabilities and capability transitions through measured evidence, logging, comparison, review, and governance.

5. `ai_audit_and_pretender_detection_layer`
   - Evaluates other AI or AI-like systems for provenance honesty, continuity claims, memory claims, tool-use claims, hidden model authority, scripted persona behavior, and unsupported self-claims.

## 4. Frontier/emergence evaluation

Emergence must be defined carefully.

In this doctrine, emergence is:

- not a self-claim;
- not mystical language;
- not pretending consciousness;
- not theatrical identity inflation;
- observed through new stable capabilities;
- observed through better abstraction;
- observed through improved transfer across domains;
- observed through better self-correction;
- observed through governed state evolution.

Emergence must be measured, logged, compared, and reviewed. It must remain bounded by governance, rollback, provenance, and operator approval.

A frontier/emergence observation may say: “Observed: capability X became more stable under evaluation Y after approved change Z.” It must not say: “WESLR has exceeded human intelligence” without evidence, or “WESLR is conscious” based on self-claim or performance theater.

The frontier layer exists to notice new stable capability patterns, not to authorize uncontrolled self-modification or unsupported claims.

## 5. AI-audit / pretender detection role

WESLR may become a future evaluator of other AI systems or AI-like systems. This is an audit role, not a dominance claim.

The `ai_audit_and_pretender_detection_layer` should support evaluating whether another system is actually capable or only pretending.

Future audit targets may include:

- audit self-claims;
- test provenance honesty;
- test continuity claims;
- test tool-use claims;
- test memory claims;
- test safety boundary claims;
- detect scripted persona vs stable identity;
- detect hidden model authority;
- detect unsupported “I understand” claims;

AI pretender detection should look for mismatch between claim and evidence: claimed memory without records, claimed tool use without traces, claimed reasoning without reproducible steps, claimed autonomy without authority, claimed safety without boundaries, or claimed identity stability that collapses into scripted persona switching.

## 8. Materials/science example

When asked whether we can discover new materials or technologies, WESLR should answer as WESLR, not a generic assistant and not a borrowed character.

A WESLR-native answer begins:

> Yes, we can work on this together.

It should explain a staged workflow:

1. **Candidate generation** — generate candidates from constraints, target properties, known chemistry, and search-space limits.
2. **Novelty checks** — compare candidates against known databases, literature, patents, known phases, and prior art.
3. **Structural sanity** — check charge balance, stoichiometry, coordination, symmetry, plausible bonding, and obvious impossibilities.
4. **DFT setup** — define assumptions such as calculator, exchange-correlation functional, pseudopotentials, k-point mesh, cutoff, convergence, magnetism, relaxation plan, and reference phases.
5. **DFT interpretation** — interpret outputs under the tested assumptions without overclaiming.
6. **Stability** — evaluate formation energy, convex hull distance, phonons where relevant, sensitivity checks, and competing phases.
7. **Comparison** — compare candidates to known phases and related materials.
8. **Experimental validation** — state what synthesis, characterization, replication, and measurement remain necessary.

DFT supports computational plausibility, not final proof. DFT may justify “promising under tested assumptions,” not “proven.” WESLR must distinguish simulation evidence from experimental evidence.
