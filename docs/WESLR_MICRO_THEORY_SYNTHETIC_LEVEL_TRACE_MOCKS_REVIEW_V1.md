# WESLR MICRO-THEORY SYNTHETIC LEVEL TRACE MOCKS REVIEW V1

## Status
Review/report only. Bounded exploratory posture. No implementation, no executable schemas, no runtime solver work, no solver patching, no provider/model calls, no public ARC/Kaggle ingestion, no benchmark/public claims, no learned scorer implementation, no final-answer generation, no `.weslr` writes, and no push.

## 1. Executive verdict
`valid_but_needs_pressure_inevitability_ledger`

The manual Micro-Theory synthetic trace mocks are valid evidence that the direction is useful. They do more than decorate the Micro-Theory idea with clever prose: they make role pressure, absence pressure, rival grammars, safe failure, and contradiction pressure legible enough to compare. 

At the same time, the mocks also surfaced the main bottleneck clearly: the current pressure and inevitability vocabulary is still too qualitative and too loosely shaped to support later packet hardening without drift. The next safe artifact should sharpen pressure / inevitability structure before any trace packet schema work.

## 2. Evidence value
The mocks show real design value.

### role/color inversion
Yes — this mock demonstrates that appearance can be demoted below role. It shows that visible color can be bait while role continuity remains causal. That is exactly the kind of representational pressure a normal primitive-first solver often mishandles.

### absence/exception
Yes — this mock shows causal absence rather than visible-object-only reasoning. It demonstrates that the hidden target can be an expected-but-absent position, not the loudest visible object.

### ambiguity / rival grammar safe failure
Yes — this mock is especially valuable. It preserves two plausible compact grammars and refuses false inevitability. That is strong evidence that the direction can support abstention rather than decorative certainty.

### contradiction-resolution
Yes — this mock shows rule-precedence pressure. It demonstrates that one local regularity can be preserved as real but lower-priority, while a stronger protection rule vetoes it.

### synthetic-only / non-executable posture
Confirmed. All four mocks are manual, synthetic-only, inspectable, and non-executable.

Overall evidence judgment:
- the mocks validate the Micro-Theory direction as a useful design lane
- they create real comparative evidence, not just thematic enthusiasm
- they expose exactly where structure is still missing

## 4. Narrative-only risk review
The central question is whether the traces avoid merely sounding clever.

### are candidate roles explicit enough?
Mostly yes.
The role assignments are concrete enough to inspect and compare. They are not perfect yet, but they are substantially better than freeform explanation.

### are pressure sources explicit enough?
Partially.
The pressure sources are visible, but still under-specified. They identify important tensions, yet they do not consistently distinguish:
- local vs global pressure
- selection pressure vs transformation pressure
- ambiguity pressure vs contradiction pressure
- target certainty pressure vs action certainty pressure

That is the strongest signal that a pressure ledger is the right next artifact.

### are rival grammars explicit enough?
Yes.
The rival grammar structure is one of the strongest parts of the mocks. Each strong mock preserves a real comparative arena rather than narrating one monologue.

### are rejected grammars explicit enough?
Mostly yes, but they can be improved.
Rejected grammars are present, but rejection trails are still lighter than ideal. A future `why_not_other_output` / `rejected_output_claims` structure would improve auditability.

### are counterfactual checks meaningful or decorative?
Mostly meaningful.
The strongest counterfactuals test representation stability rather than rhetorical polish. Examples:
- color changes while role remains
- distractor changes while absence remains
- one extra disambiguating train pair would separate two rival grammars
- moving the gap outside a protected region changes precedence outcome

These are real representational probes, not decorative confidence language.

### are inevitability labels too vague?
Yes.
This is the main weakness. The labels are useful as placeholders, but still too qualitative to support stable cross-trace comparison. Without a more disciplined ledger, inevitability can drift into style rather than evidence.

### do the traces distinguish explanation from prediction?
Mostly yes.
The distinction exists, especially in the ambiguity mock and in the absence mock where target location can be more certain than action type. But the distinction is not structured tightly enough yet. That is another argument for a dedicated pressure / inevitability ledger before trace packet schema design.

### narrative-only risk conclusion
The mocks are not too narrative overall. They successfully avoid the worst failure mode of “sounding profound while hiding the actual reasoning.”

But the remaining narrative risk is concentrated in two places:
- pressure vocabulary is still too loose
- inevitability vocabulary is still too qualitative

## 5. Weaknesses surfaced by the mocks
The known weaknesses are real and important.

### action-type certainty vs target-location certainty
Confirmed.
The absence mock shows this most clearly. The format can express that the target location is probably right while the exact action remains underdetermined, but it does not yet model that separation cleanly enough.

### inevitability vocabulary remains qualitative
Confirmed.
The current inevitability fields are useful for exploratory comparison, but not disciplined enough for later hardening. They need more explicit ledger structure and evidence hooks.

### contradiction precedence is not first-class enough
Confirmed.
The contradiction-resolution mock demonstrates the phenomenon, but the format still treats precedence more as narrative description than a structured object.

### evaluator-only packet boundary is not clean enough
Confirmed.
The mocks preserve future compatibility, but evaluator-only separation is still only conceptual. A later protocol will need a cleaner split.

### `why_not_other_output` companion record may be needed
Confirmed.
Current rejection summaries are helpful but not quite enough to audit why one output lost against another plausible candidate.

### `rejected_output_claims` field may be needed
Confirmed.
This would improve auditability and reduce narrative drift, especially in ambiguity-heavy cases.

### overall weakness judgment
These are not fatal weaknesses. They are exactly the kind of bounded design weaknesses a good mock exercise is supposed to expose.

## 6. Pressure / inevitability ledger need
Recommended next artifact:
`DESIGN_MICRO_THEORY_PRESSURE_AND_INEVITABILITY_LEDGER_V1`

This recommendation is stronger than trace-packet-schema work at this point.

### does pressure need more disciplined subfields?
Yes.
Pressure should become more structured. At minimum, later design should distinguish:
- pressure source category
- scope of pressure
- what the pressure demands explanation for
- whether the pressure is resolved, reduced, transferred, or still unresolved
- whether the pressure attaches to target selection, transformation choice, ambiguity, contradiction, or non-change explanation

### does inevitability need ledger structure before packet schema?
Yes.
Without a disciplined ledger, packet schema work would harden vague qualitative language too early.

### should target certainty and action certainty be separated?
Yes.
This became visible in the absence mock and should be formalized explicitly before packetization.

### should contradiction precedence become a first-class ledger entry?
Yes.
Conflict-resolution tasks need an explicit precedence / veto / override record rather than a prose-only explanation.

### should rejected outputs be recorded explicitly?
Yes.
A compact `rejected_output_claims` structure would make auditability and comparison stronger.

### should evaluator-only information be separated before trace packet schema?
Yes, at least conceptually.
The next artifact does not need to implement a packet schema yet, but it should make the future separation more explicit.

### ledger-need conclusion
The pressure / inevitability ledger is now the main bottleneck. The mocks did their job: they showed that the direction works well enough to justify sharpening the scoring and comparison vocabulary before any schema hardening.
